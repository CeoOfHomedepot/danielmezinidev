01

The challenge

Manage NPC simulation and animation while reducing the overhead of the engine's built-in humanoid and physics systems.

02

What I built

A server-authoritative entity architecture with a custom rendering, joint, and animation pipeline. Byte-level buffer serialization carries updates to clients at 10 Hz.

03

Rendering approach

The client uses Workspace:BulkMoveTo() for batched movement, alongside off-screen and distance culling to limit visual work.

Read the engineering notes +

Separating simulation from presentation

The server owns entity logic, while the client handles how those entities appear. This separates gameplay decisions from the cost of drawing and animating the world.

Working at the engine boundary

Replacing built-in humanoid behavior means taking on responsibility for joints, animation, and rendering. The custom pipeline gives more control over that work, with additional implementation complexity.

Performance evidence

The linked Roblox experience is the demonstration. Reproducible benchmark results and project source are not published here yet.

02 / Backend specialization

The systems behind the game.

I build the backend systems that connect player data, shared worlds, and gameplay rules. My approach centers on server-side validation, persistent data, and a clear separation between game logic and client presentation.

01

Inventory systems

I built inventories around persistent player data, with the server validating item ownership and requested changes. The backend manages inventory state, while the client presents the player's items and available actions.

02

Player trading

I handled trading through server-side checks on both players' offers. Validating the items and each player's side of the exchange keeps the transfer tied to the actual inventory state, rather than trusting what the client submits.

03

Guilds & roles

I organized guild systems around membership, roles, and permissions. The server checks a player's authority before applying guild actions, with persistent data keeping the group and its members connected across sessions.

04

Combat systems

I kept combat calculations, state, and cooldown checks on the server, while handling visuals on the client. That separation let me focus on responsive, smooth presentation while the backend remained responsible for gameplay outcomes.

05

World handling

I built world and zone management around server-owned state, coordinating the logic that runs across different parts of the game. Keeping world responsibilities organized helps connect NPCs, events, and player interactions.

06

Quests, events & NPCs

I developed modular systems for quest branching, dynamic events, and NPC interactions and navigation. Breaking those responsibilities into reusable components gave the RPG a foundation for connecting gameplay features as the world expanded.
